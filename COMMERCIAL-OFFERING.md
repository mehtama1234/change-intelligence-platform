# Commercial offering

## Positioning

The product is **evidence-backed change intelligence**.

It sits between a trend newsletter, a market-research firm, a company-data
platform, and a strategy advisory practice. Its difference is that it connects
early signals to industry economics, company movement, and real-world effects.

## Initial customer

Start with teams that regularly need to understand change before the market has
settled on a simple story:

- corporate strategy and innovation teams;
- investment and diligence teams;
- product and market-intelligence teams;
- policy, risk, and public-affairs teams;
- research and consulting groups.

## Initial product wedge

Launch with one deep domain rather than selling the entire universe at once.
Good candidates include:

- AI, work, and enterprise control;
- energy and infrastructure buildout;
- healthcare access and administrative burden;
- household affordability and consumer power.

The internal system remains broad. The first paid product is narrow and useful.

## Product tiers

### Intelligence subscription

Weekly changes, monthly theme briefings, quarterly company updates, source
traces, evidence boundaries, and watchlist alerts.

### Team workspace

Private watchlists, competitors, themes, saved questions, shared briefings,
commentary, and organization-specific monitoring.

### Custom research

Market-entry studies, competitor mapping, emerging-company scans, policy and
infrastructure monitoring, and board-ready evidence briefings.

### Data and API access

Structured records for changes, companies, industries, themes, evidence
chains, source passages, open questions, and historical movement.

### Advisory services

Workshops and recurring research partnerships using the same evidence system.

## Why customers pay

Customers are not paying for more articles. They pay for reduced uncertainty:

- faster orientation;
- a shared view across fragmented sources;
- earlier detection of meaningful change;
- fewer unsupported strategic assumptions;
- clear alternatives and risks;
- reusable, auditable briefings;
- an institutional memory that survives staff turnover.

## Trust requirements

Every commercial insight must show:

- what is directly known;
- what is management-reported;
- what is calculated;
- what is inferred;
- what remains missing;
- what could prove it wrong.

Trust is the product, not an appendix.
