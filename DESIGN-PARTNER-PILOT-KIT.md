# Design-partner pilot kit

This is the operating guide for the first three real design partners. It turns
the product's pilot records into a shared working agreement. It is not a sales
promise and it is not a way to manufacture a positive result.

## What the partner is agreeing to test

The partner chooses one decision that is difficult because the evidence is
spread across reports, markets, companies, people, and changing conditions.
The service sends a bounded, source-linked update on an agreed schedule. The
partner checks the evidence, says what was useful or wrong, and records whether
the update informed a decision.

The pilot is successful only if the product makes the partner's real research
work better. A polished briefing, a large source count, or a prepared delivery
is not success by itself.

## Choose the right partner

Select three teams in one first domain. Each should:

- make the chosen kind of decision at least once during the pilot;
- already spend time collecting scattered evidence;
- have one person who can review each delivery within the agreed window;
- be willing to show how the research was used, including when it was not useful;
- be able to share only the private material that is necessary.

Do not select a partner who only wants a one-time report, cannot name a real
decision, or expects the service to make the decision for them.

## Before the first delivery

The operator and partner must agree on these items in writing:

1. The decision question: what decision should become faster, clearer, or less
   uncertain?
2. The scope: which themes, companies, industries, places, and private sources
   are in or out?
3. The schedule: weekly, monthly, or quarterly, and the review deadline.
4. The success measures: no more than three, written so a person can judge
   them after each delivery.
5. The reviewers: owner, researcher, and any viewer who needs access.
6. The data boundary: what the partner may submit and what must stay outside
   the system.
7. The correction path: where an inaccurate, incomplete, stale, or wrongly
   scoped reading is reported.
8. The pilot end point: the minimum number of reviewed deliveries and the date
   of the human improve/continue/expand/stop checkpoint.

The operator then provisions the workspace, maps identities through the
identity provider, records invitations, and waits for confirmation. A workspace
is not active merely because it exists. The onboarding checklist must show the
decision question, evidence scope, success measures, cadence, saved question,
and first handoff state.

## The cycle for every delivery

### 1. Prepare the evidence

The system checks source availability, captures changed inputs, preserves
source versions, and records the refresh run. Missing, stale, or unavailable
sources stay visible. They hold the affected handoff when the missing evidence
could change the reading.

### 2. Review before sharing

The researcher checks the briefing, its evidence chain, the strongest
alternative explanation, and the next test. The researcher either publishes a
bounded handoff or holds it for correction or re-review. No generated wording
may be treated as independently verified just because it sounds confident.

### 3. Deliver and acknowledge

The partner receives the source-linked handoff through the approved channel.
The delivery notification records whether it was delivered or acknowledged.
The operator investigates pending, failed, or dead-lettered notifications
before treating the handoff as delivered.

### 4. Partner review

Within the agreed review window, the partner records:

- whether the delivery was useful, unclear, or not useful;
- whether it informed or changed a decision;
- whether anything was inaccurate, missing, unclear, or aimed at the wrong
  scope;
- which success measures were met, partly met, not met, or unknown;
- a plain-language note describing what happened;
- the next question or test.

The partner should open the source and evidence chain before submitting the
review. The system records that activity separately from the partner's opinion.

## Measures and their exact meaning

The scorecard reports recorded activity, not business causation:

| Measure | Meaning | What it cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source-trace inspections | How often evidence behind a claim was opened | That the evidence was accepted or correct |
| Briefing reuse | How many published briefings were recorded as part of a decision | That the briefing caused the decision |
| False alerts | Alerts the partner marked as noise | The quality of every alert not reviewed |
| Alert correction response | Time from an alert to a correction disposition | The time needed to fix the underlying research |
| Delivery review time | Time from handoff generation to partner review | The time to make a broader business decision |
| Time to answer | Partner-reported time spent answering the agreed question after a handoff | That the answer was correct or caused a decision |
| Missed important changes | A partner report that the delivery failed to include an important change | The quality of changes the partner did not notice or report |
| Useful deliveries | Deliveries marked useful | General product-market fit |
| Decision impact | Deliveries marked as informing or changing a decision | A causal business result |
| Later result | Whether the partner later marked the reading held, changed, wrong, or unknown | A measured outcome outside the workspace |

“Not measured” is a valid result. The service must not convert silence into a
positive result or compare partners whose scopes and decision questions are not
compatible.

## Researcher and operator responsibilities

### Researcher

- maintain source and evidence quality;
- review changed or stale evidence before publication;
- explain what is directly known, calculated, reported, inferred, and missing;
- record corrections without rewriting history;
- propose the next test when the evidence is weak;
- keep private partner material inside its workspace.

### Operator

- keep identity, invitations, schedules, backups, and notification routes
  working;
- use the operator overview and cohort report for aggregate health;
- investigate held deliveries, failed refreshes, source outages, and dead
  letters;
- download kickoff and closeout packets when a partner review requires them;
- never expose customer notes or private source details in a cohort report;
- record the human checkpoint decision and its next step.

### Partner

- provide a real decision question and review each delivery;
- inspect evidence before relying on a claim;
- report useful and failed readings honestly;
- identify corrections and missing context;
- record what decision changed, was informed, deferred, or untouched.

## Checkpoint after three reviewed deliveries

Do not hold the checkpoint until there are at least three reviewed deliveries,
unless the pilot is being stopped for a safety, privacy, or service failure.
Before the meeting, both sides read the closeout packet and answer:

1. Did the service help with the agreed decision question?
2. Did the partner return and review the evidence more than once?
3. Were false alerts and corrections manageable?
4. Did the evidence chain make disagreement easier to resolve?
5. Did any recorded decision change or become clearer?
6. Which success measure failed or remains unknown?
7. What should be tested next?

The partner or authorized owner records exactly one human choice:

- **Improve** — keep the pilot narrow and fix a named problem;
- **Continue** — run more cycles before deciding on a broader offer;
- **Expand** — agree on a paid recurring scope and any new users or domains;
- **Stop** — record why, preserve the history, and close delivery safely.

The system may suggest a readiness state from recorded observations, but it
must never make this commercial choice silently.

## Evidence to retain

For each partner, retain the workspace export, kickoff packet, delivery
history, review records, correction notes, readiness history, closeout packet,
and the human checkpoint decision. Preserve the source and audit history under
the product's retention and deletion rules.

## Pilot completion gate

The first three-partner pilot is ready for a commercial decision only when:

- each partner has an isolated workspace and a named decision question;
- each partner received at least one source-linked recurring handoff;
- each delivery has a recorded review or a documented reason it could not be
  reviewed;
- source-trace use, briefing reuse, false alerts, correction response, and
  delivery review time are measured where observations exist;
- at least one partner records a real decision informed or changed by a
  briefing, unless the human checkpoint explicitly stops the pilot for a
  documented reason;
- all corrections and source limitations remain visible;
- the operator can explain the state of every partner without opening private
  customer notes;
- every partner receives an improve/continue/expand/stop checkpoint.

If any item is missing, the pilot is incomplete. Do not describe it as product
validation; describe the exact missing observation.
