# Product specification

## Product promise

Give a strategy, investment, policy, or research team a continuously updated
answer to five practical questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. Which companies, industries, and people are affected?
4. Who captures the benefit and who absorbs the cost or work?
5. What should we watch next?

The answer must use ordinary language and open every important statement to
its source.

## What the product is

The platform combines six views of change:

1. **Early signals** — new behavior, language, products, events, and cultural signals.
2. **Lived conditions** — household, worker, customer, community, and institutional effects.
3. **Industry structure** — market size, participants, bottlenecks, substitutes, and economics.
4. **Company movement** — annual reports, quarterly reports, guidance, capital allocation, and operating results.
5. **New business formation** — private-company growth, customer pain, and emerging solutions.
6. **Independent testing** — public records, research, outcomes, contradictions, and failed proof paths.

## Core user journey

```text
Choose a theme, company, industry, place, or question
→ orient in the current evidence
→ see what changed since the previous period
→ inspect the mechanism and affected groups
→ compare companies or alternative explanations
→ open the exact source passage or measurement
→ save a bounded question or briefing
→ receive a refresh when the evidence changes
```

## Main frontend surfaces

### Change feed

Shows important new changes rather than every new document. Each item states
the change, why it matters, source date, evidence status, and next question.

### Theme atlas

Starts with a broad issue such as AI and work, household affordability,
healthcare access, infrastructure, trust, or aging. The user can move from
theme to mechanism, industry, company, affected group, and outcome evidence.

### Company and industry pages

Each page explains the operating model, annual structure, quarterly movement,
financial quality, strategy changes, risks, outside evidence, and open tests.

### Change timeline

Places management statements, quarterly results, operating events, external
records, and outcome evidence on separate but comparable time rails.

### Evidence chain

Shows the path from signal to mechanism to response to affected group to
outcome. Each step has its own source and claim limit.

### Evidence coverage

Shows which source families are present, partial, or missing for the current
theme. It separately counts public-company annual-plus-quarterly histories and
does not treat a missing outcome record as proof of an outcome. This keeps a
thin evidence base visible instead of allowing a polished summary to hide it.

### Briefing studio

Lets a user select evidence, arrange a bounded story, add a question, export
the result, and reopen it later without losing provenance.

After a briefing is published, the user can record whether it affected a
decision and later mark whether the reading held, changed, was wrong, or is
still unknown. This creates a measured feedback loop without pretending that
one customer's experience is a general outcome.

### Watchlist and alerts

Tracks selected companies, industries, themes, policies, and evidence gaps.
Alerts are triggered by meaningful changes, not just new page counts.

### Pilot scorecard

For each customer workspace, the product records a small operating scorecard:
how many alerts were reviewed, how many were useful or false, how quickly they
were answered, how often briefings were exported or used in a decision, and
what users later recorded about the reading. The scorecard measures the
customer's recorded experience. It is not a claim that the product caused a
business result or that one workspace proves general market value.

## Insight types

The system should produce insights about:

- how a company really makes money;
- what changed in the latest quarter;
- whether growth is price, volume, mix, acquisition, or temporary scarcity;
- whether earnings turn into cash;
- where capital and operating pressure are building;
- how management language changes relative to results;
- how an industry bottleneck moves through companies;
- where customer, worker, supplier, household, or public burden lands;
- which emerging private companies reveal new pain or new solutions;
- whether outside evidence supports or weakens the company story;
- what evidence is missing and what would settle the question.

## Non-goals

The platform must not become:

- an automatic investment recommendation engine;
- a single company score or “moat” ranking;
- a trend-volume leaderboard;
- a generic chatbot with no source trace;
- a causal claim generator that joins incompatible datasets;
- a replacement for professional legal, financial, medical, or policy judgment.
