# First domain brief: AI, work, and enterprise control

## Plain-language question

When companies introduce AI into work, does it mainly give people useful
capacity, or does it move control over tasks, measurement, scheduling, and
appeals into systems that workers and customers cannot easily see or change?

## Why this is the first vertical slice

This domain already has useful material in every connected research source:

- early AI adoption and product signals;
- worker, customer, household, and institutional evidence;
- industry structure and infrastructure requirements;
- fast-growing private companies selling AI services;
- public-company investment, workforce, and quarterly disclosures;
- independent outcome, remedy, and recourse records.

It is broad enough to prove the platform's purpose but bounded enough to
complete a first customer journey.

## Required evidence path

```text
AI capability or adoption signal
→ task or workflow changes
→ who controls the decision and who can challenge it
→ company investment, product, or labor response
→ worker/customer/institution exposure
→ measured result or implementation record
→ remedy, correction, switching, or exit
→ strongest counterexample
```

## First company and industry set

The first fixture uses source-linked records from the existing repositories;
it is not a claim that these companies are directly comparable on every metric.
The initial production candidate set should include:

- one AI infrastructure or semiconductor company;
- one enterprise software or workflow company;
- one services or labor-intermediation company;
- one customer-facing platform;
- one company with a documented external control or remedy case.

The selection gate is economic contrast and evidence quality, not popularity.

## First customer jobs

1. A strategy lead wants to know whether AI investment is changing the work
   system or only the software budget.
2. A product leader wants to know where customers gain convenience and where
   they lose recourse or human access.
3. A risk or policy lead wants to know which control points, affected groups,
   and correction paths are actually documented.

## Initial open questions

- Does reported AI access reduce work, or move work into review, correction,
  training, and exception handling?
- Which companies control the workflow rather than merely supply a model?
- When an automated decision is wrong, who can correct it and how quickly?
- Do customers and workers have a practical way to switch or leave?
- Which benefits appear in quarterly company results, and which costs appear
  only in worker, customer, or institutional evidence?
